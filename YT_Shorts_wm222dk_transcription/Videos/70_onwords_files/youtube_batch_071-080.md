### Claims identified & fact-check

**Claim 1:** ADHD begins as a coping mechanism in which a child’s brain tunes out in response to early stress, and this mechanism becomes programmed into the brain  
→ **Unsupported** — early adversity and stress are associated with ADHD and may influence symptom severity, but current evidence does not establish this as the general origin of ADHD. ADHD has a predominantly genetic, multifactorial aetiology, and environmental associations should not be converted into a universal trauma-based causal mechanism.

**Claim 2:** ADHD is not inherited  
→ **Unsupported** — ADHD is highly heritable and has a predominantly genetic aetiology involving many common and rare genetic variants. It is not inherited through a single gene in every case, but denying inherited influence contradicts strong evidence.

**Claim 3:** ADHD-related absent-mindedness and scattered attention are not manifestations of a neurodevelopmental disorder  
→ **Unsupported** — ADHD is formally classified as a neurodevelopmental disorder. Describing it as a coping response does not replace the diagnostic requirement that persistent inattention and/or hyperactivity-impulsivity cause clinically significant impairment.

### Claims identified & fact-check

**Claim 1:** Centanafadine (Simtriyo) is a newly FDA-approved stimulant medication for ADHD  
→ **Supported** — the FDA approved Simtriyo (centanafadine) on 24 July 2026 for ADHD. The prescribing information describes it as a central nervous system stimulant.

**Claim 2:** Centanafadine inhibits the reuptake of norepinephrine, dopamine, and serotonin and belongs to the NDSRI class  
→ **Supported** — the FDA prescribing information describes centanafadine as a norepinephrine-dopamine-serotonin reuptake inhibitor with inhibitory activity at all three transporters.

**Claim 3:** The medication being discussed is serdexmethylphenidate, the generic drug in Azstarys  
→ **Unsupported** — this identifies a different medication. Simtriyo contains centanafadine, whereas Azstarys contains serdexmethylphenidate and dexmethylphenidate. Azstarys was FDA-approved in 2021, not newly approved in July 2026.

→ **Supported** — this matches the FDA indication.

**Claim 5:** The recommended adult starting dose is 210 mg each morning and may be increased to a maximum of 280 mg daily  
→ **Supported** — this matches the FDA adult dosing instructions.

**Claim 6:** Increasing norepinephrine produces alertness and attention, increasing dopamine improves motivation and reward processing, and increasing serotonin probably improves impulsivity and emotional control  
→ **Unsupported** — centanafadine acts at all three reuptake transporters, but the FDA states that its mechanism of action in ADHD remains unclear. The video presents simplified, transmitter-by-transmitter clinical effects—especially the proposed serotonin effects—as though they were established.

**Claim 7:** Decreased appetite, insomnia, nausea, and dry mouth are side effects of Simtriyo  
→ **Supported with qualification** — all four were reported in trials and are among the common adverse reactions in adults, although the most common reactions differ by age group.

**Claim 8:** At the time of the video, Simtriyo’s federal controlled-substance schedule had not yet been assigned, although scheduling was expected  
→ **Supported** — the initial FDA label stated that the controlled-substance schedule was pending and warned of abuse, misuse, and addiction risks.

**Title:** ADHD + Autism = AuDHD. Here's what that can really feel like…

**Transcript:**

> ADHD and autism frequently co-occur. Many people with one or the two diagnoses show elevated traits of both ADHD and autism. Common experiences for ADHD and autism include sensory differences, intense focus on specific interests, rejection sensitivity, executive dysfunction, sleep issues, and emotional regulation. Someone with ADHD is more likely to seek out novelty and make more impulsive decisions, whereas an autistic person is more likely to crave routine, structure, and order. If someone is autistic and has ADHD, they’re known as AuDHD. They may experience an internal struggle and tension between their competing autistic and ADHD traits and a heightened experience of shared traits.

### Claims identified & fact-check

**Claim 1:** ADHD and autism frequently co-occur  
→ **Supported** — co-diagnosis is well established. Reviews report substantial ADHD prevalence among autistic children and elevated autism prevalence among children with ADHD.

**Claim 2:** People diagnosed with either condition may show elevated traits associated with the other  
→ **Supported** — ADHD and autism have distinct diagnostic criteria but show meaningful phenotypic overlap, and clinically significant traits of the other condition may occur without necessarily satisfying all criteria for a second diagnosis.

**Claim 3:** Sensory differences, executive dysfunction, sleep problems, and emotional-regulation difficulties can occur in both ADHD and autism  
→ **Supported** — these features are documented across both conditions, although none is present in every affected person and some are not core diagnostic criteria.

**Claim 4:** Intense focus on particular interests can occur in both ADHD and autism  
→ **Supported with qualification** — restricted or highly focused interests are characteristic of autism, while intense task engagement or “hyperfocus” is reported in ADHD research and clinical accounts; the features are not necessarily identical in mechanism or presentation.

**Claim 5:** Rejection sensitivity is a common feature of both ADHD and autism  
→ **Unsupported** — distress following rejection can occur, but “rejection sensitivity” or “rejection-sensitive dysphoria” is not a formal diagnostic feature of either condition, and the evidence does not justify presenting it as a general shared characteristic.

**Claim 6:** People with ADHD are more likely to seek novelty and make impulsive decisions, whereas autistic people are more likely to prefer routine, structure, and order  
→ **Supported with qualification** — impulsivity is a core ADHD domain, and insistence on sameness, routines, or restricted patterns is central to autism. However, these are group-level tendencies and should not be treated as universal or mutually exclusive.

**Claim 7:** “AuDHD” is a term for a person who has both autism and ADHD  
→ **Supported with qualification** — AuDHD is a widely used informal community term for co-occurring autism and ADHD, but it is not a separate DSM-5-TR or ICD-11 diagnosis.

**Claim 8:** Co-occurring autism and ADHD can produce tension between apparently competing traits and a heightened burden from overlapping difficulties  
→ **Supported with qualification** — the “internal tension” wording describes subjective experience, but research supports greater functional impairment and often more severe difficulties in people with both conditions than in people with only one.
