Claims identified & fact-check:

Claim 1: Loud chewers send you into a "fit of rage"
→ Unsupported — sound sensitivity/misophonia is not a diagnostic criterion for ADHD in DSM-5-TR or ICD-11; it is more commonly associated with sensory sensitivities in autism spectrum disorder or a separate condition (misophonia), not ADHD specifically
Claim 2: Disliking "big spoons" for no clear reason
→ Unsupported — a sensory/texture preference quirk with no basis in ADHD diagnostic criteria
Claim 3: Catastrophizing and needing reassurance when someone asks for a "quick chat" with no context
→ Unsupported — this reflects anxiety-driven catastrophic thinking, which ICD-11 explicitly distinguishes from ADHD (anxiety and fear-related disorders are a separate diagnostic category, and co-occurring anxiety should not be counted as core ADHD symptomatology)
Claim 4: A minor afternoon commitment prevents doing anything else all day
→ Partially supported — this loosely reflects difficulty with time perception/planning around tasks (a recognized executive-function-related struggle in ICD-11's inattention criteria), though framed here as an absolute, universal experience rather than a variable planning difficulty
Claim 5: Becoming obsessed with someone/something and disappearing from friends for months (hyperfocus)
→ Partially supported — consistent with ICD-11's note that inattention may not be evident during intensely stimulating/rewarding activities, which underlies the "hyperfocus" phenomenon; however, "disappearing from friends for months" is an exaggerated, generalized extension not found in the diagnostic criteria
Claim 6: Having thousands of unviewed screenshots on your phone
→ Unsupported — a specific modern-life anecdote with no grounding in ADHD diagnostic criteria, though loosely evocative of disorganization
Claim 7: Struggling to clean alone but suddenly finding it easy when a friend visits
→ Unsupported — not a recognized ADHD criterion or documented clinical pattern in the reference materials
Claim 8: Tiny criticism creates overwhelming anger or sadness
→ Unsupported — resembles "rejection sensitive dysphoria," a popular but non-official construct not included in DSM-5-TR or ICD-11 diagnostic criteria for ADHD
Claim 9: Getting excited buying a new notebook but never using it
→ Unsupported — anecdotal novelty-seeking behavior with no diagnostic basis
Claim 10: Feeling no sense of accomplishment after achieving something, only relief that it's done
→ Unsupported — not a recognized ADHD symptom in the reference materials; overlaps loosely with emotional dysregulation but is presented as a specific, universal experience without clinical grounding
