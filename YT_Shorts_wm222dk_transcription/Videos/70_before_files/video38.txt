Claims identified & fact-check:

Claim 1: There's no clinical test for ADHD — no blood test, no CT scan
→ Supported — NICE's diagnostic process (1.3.1) relies on full clinical/psychosocial assessment, developmental and psychiatric history, and observer reports; 1.3.2 explicitly states diagnosis should not be made solely on rating scale or observational data, and no blood test, CT scan, or other biomarker test is listed anywhere in NICE or ICD-11 as part of the diagnostic process

Claim 2: ADHD diagnosis is "based on someone's opinion about your child," implying it's merely subjective rather than a legitimate clinical determination
→ Unsupported/mischaracterized — this overstates and misrepresents the diagnostic process. NICE 1.3.1 requires diagnosis to be made only by "a specialist psychiatrist, paediatrician or other appropriately qualified healthcare professional with training and expertise," based on a full clinical/psychosocial assessment, structured developmental/psychiatric history, and observer reports across settings — a structured clinical evaluation process, not mere personal opinion

Claim 3: Using medication for a behavior problem doesn't solve the problem, it just masks a symptom
→ Unsupported — this contradicts the evidence-based framing in NICE, which presents medication as a clinically effective treatment for ADHD symptoms and impairment (1.5.13, 1.5.15, 1.7.27 — dose titration is guided toward "reduced symptoms, positive behaviour change, improvements in education, employment and relationships"), not as symptom-masking; ADHD is also classified as a neurodevelopmental disorder (ICD-11) rather than a "behavior problem," so this framing misrepresents both the condition and the treatment's evidenced purpose

Claim 4: There are always side effects (implying medication universally causes adverse effects)
→ Partially supported — NICE does extensively document potential adverse effects of ADHD medications (weight/height changes, cardiovascular effects, tics, sleep changes, sexual dysfunction with atomoxetine, etc. — sections 1.8.1–1.8.19), so acknowledging side effects exist has a basis; however, "always" overstates it, since NICE frames these as effects to monitor and manage, not universal or inevitable outcomes for every individual
