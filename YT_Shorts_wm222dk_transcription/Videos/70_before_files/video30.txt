Claims identified & fact-check:

Claim 1: There are two potential presentations of ADHD — inattentive type and hyperactive/impulsive type
→ Supported — ICD-11 lists exactly these specifiers: predominantly inattentive presentation (6A05.0) and predominantly hyperactive-impulsive presentation (6A05.1)

Claim 2: Inattentive-type criteria include things like being unable to complete tasks, struggling to follow verbal instructions, and losing things
→ Supported — matches ICD-11 inattention clusters: "not completing tasks," "often seeming not to listen when spoken to directly," and "losing things"

Claim 3: Hyperactive/impulsive-type criteria include talking a lot, struggling to wait your turn in a queue, and being unable to sit still even where expected to
→ Supported — matches ICD-11 hyperactivity-impulsivity clusters: "talking too much," "difficulty waiting their turn... in conversation, games or activities," and "difficulty sitting still without fidgeting"/discomfort with sitting still

Claim 4: You need to "tick six" traits within a section to receive that presentation's diagnosis
→ Unsupported in the attached references — ICD-11 (the reference material provided) describes symptoms qualitatively ("several symptoms... typically from the following clusters") without specifying a numeric threshold like six; this specific "six symptoms" count reflects DSM-5 criteria rather than anything stated in the attached ICD-11 documentation, so it is not supported by the prescribed evidence base as given

Claim 5: Meeting six criteria in both sections results in a "combined type" diagnosis, meaning both presentations are present
→ Supported — matches ICD-11's combined presentation (6A05.2): "both hyperactive-impulsive and inattentive symptoms are clinically significant aspects... with neither clearly predominating"

Claim 6: If you are an adult, it needs to be considered to have at least a moderate impact on your life (e.g., struggling to keep a job or maintain relationships)
→ Partially supported — NICE 1.3.3 does require symptoms to "cause at least moderate psychological, social, or educational or occupational impairment," which matches the substance of the claim; however, NICE applies this moderate-impairment threshold to ADHD diagnosis generally, not as an adult-specific add-on requirement as the video implies
