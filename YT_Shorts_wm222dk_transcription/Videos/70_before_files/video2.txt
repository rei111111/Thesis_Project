Claims identified & fact-check:

Claim 1: People with ADHD experience "burnout" more quickly than neurotypical people
→ Unsupported — "ADHD burnout" is not a recognized diagnostic construct in DSM-5-TR or ICD-11, and no systematic review evidence is cited; this is a popularized claim without support in the prescribed reference base
Claim 2: Morning overwhelm from tasks (inbox), decision paralysis over clothing, and forgetting items (keys)
→ Supported — matches ICD-11 inattention symptoms: losing things, difficulty planning/organizing tasks, being easily overwhelmed by low-structure demands
Claim 3: Anxious rumination about lateness and explaining it to a boss, framed as an ADHD-driven pattern
→ Partially supported / overgeneralized — the underlying time-management/organizational difficulty is consistent with inattention criteria, but the anxious rumination is presented as an inherent, universal ADHD trait rather than a possible co-occurring anxiety feature (ICD-11 notes anxiety as a common but separate comorbidity, not a core ADHD symptom)
Claim 4: ADHD people "prepare an alter ego" and force eye contact to get through social interactions at work
→ Unsupported / misattributed — forced eye contact and social "masking" are more characteristic of autism spectrum disorder's social-communication profile; ICD-11 explicitly distinguishes ADHD from ASD on this basis, so presenting this as a defining ADHD experience conflates the two conditions
Claim 5: Constant rumination over whether something said in a meeting "sounded stupid"
→ Unsupported — reflects a popular but non-diagnostic construct (sometimes called "rejection sensitive dysphoria"), which does not appear in DSM-5-TR or ICD-11 as an ADHD criterion
Claim 6: Nagging worry about whether they locked the front door, framed as an ADHD trait
→ Unsupported / misattributed — this resembles obsessive-compulsive checking behavior; ICD-11 notes ADHD carries an increased risk of co-occurring OCD, but checking rituals are not themselves an ADHD symptom
