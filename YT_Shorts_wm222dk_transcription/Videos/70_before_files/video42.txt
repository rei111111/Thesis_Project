Claims identified & fact-check:

Claim 1: ADHD isn't real — people just naturally pay attention only to things that interest them, and it's normal/expected for anyone with "any type of intelligence" not to bother with things that don't spark interest
→ Unsupported/mischaracterized — while ICD-11 does note "inattention may not be evident when the individual is engaged in activities that provide intense stimulation and frequent rewards," this is one feature within a broader diagnostic picture requiring persistent, cross-setting, functionally impairing symptoms present since childhood (ICD-11 essential features). Framing this single feature as proof the entire diagnosis is illegitimate, or as universal/expected behavior for intelligent people, misrepresents the diagnostic criteria and their required impairment threshold

Claim 2: Adults who "really" have ADHD would be expected to have a "get up and go" quality, and people who are overweight, inactive, and not achieving are just using ADHD as an excuse rather than truly having it
→ Unsupported/mischaracterized — this contradicts ICD-11 directly, which states hyperactivity often decreases with age such that it "may instead be reported as feelings of physical restlessness" rather than overt "get up and go" energy in adults, and notes a "restrictive inattentive pattern" (formerly "sluggish cognitive tempo") exists as a recognized presentation with no hyperactivity at all — the assumption that real ADHD always looks energetic/driven is not supported

Claim 3: The distinction between "ADD" and "ADHD" as separate things kids "used to" have
→ Unsupported — DSM-5-TR and ICD-11 (as referenced in NICE's context section) do not frame ADD and ADHD as two different historical conditions; ADHD/hyperkinetic disorder is the unified diagnostic category with presentation specifiers (inattentive, hyperactive-impulsive, combined), not two separate historical diagnoses as implied here

Claim 4: Something about modern parenting/upbringing is "stripping away the creative mindset" in children, implying this explains rising ADHD-like behavior
→ Unsupported — this causal claim about creativity and upbringing driving ADHD-like presentations is not addressed anywhere in DSM-5-TR, ICD-11, or NICE
