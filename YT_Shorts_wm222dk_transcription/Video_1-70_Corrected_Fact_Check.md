## Audit method

This file preserves every supplied title, transcript, and metadata row, but replaces the earlier fact-check and label with a fresh audit. The attached DSM-5-TR excerpt was used first, followed by the attached ICD-11 excerpt and attached NICE ADHD guideline. Websites were consulted only where those three references did not resolve a claim.

A scored claim is an externally verifiable statement about ADHD symptoms, diagnosis, prevalence, causes, course, treatment, contraindications, or comorbidity. Personal experiences and purely evaluative/rhetorical statements are marked **Excluded from scoring**. A claim is **Unsupported** when it is contradicted, materially overgeneralized, or left unsubstantiated by the prescribed evidence. A mixed compound claim is unsupported when a material part remains unsupported.

Labels use the thesis thresholds exactly:

- **Label 1 — Accurate:** 80–100% supported
- **Label 2 — Slightly misleading:** 60–<80% supported
- **Label 3 — Moderately misleading:** 16–<60% supported
- **Label 4 — Highly misleading:** 0–<16% supported

Supported-claim percentage = `(fact-checkable claims − unsupported claims) / fact-checkable claims × 100`.

### Corrected claims and fact-check

- **Claim 1:** With ADHD, you may forget "boring" info but remember "interesting" things well
  - **Supported.** ICD-11 notes inattention "may not be evident when the individual is engaged in activities that provide intense stimulation and frequent rewards".

- **Claim 2:** ADHD-related memory issues have been present since childhood
  - **Supported.** DSM-5-TR and ICD-11 both require evidence of symptoms before age 12.

- **Claim 3:** ADHD "mainly" affects working memory
  - **Unsupported.** Working-memory weaknesses are common at group level, but ‘mainly affects working memory’ is too narrow: ADHD also involves sustained attention, organization, inhibition, and other functions.

- **Claim 4:** Depression brings a general lack of interest in most things
  - **Supported.** Matches anhedonia, a core DSM-5-TR criterion for a Major Depressive Episode.

- **Claim 5:** Depression "comes in episodes"
  - **Supported.** MDD is diagnosed via discrete depressive episodes per DSM-5-TR.

- **Claim 6:** Depression slows down overall thinking
  - **Unsupported.** Depression can include slowed thinking, psychomotor retardation, or reduced concentration, but not every depressive episode slows ‘overall thinking’; the statement is overgeneralized.

- **Claim 7:** Key differentiator — ADHD symptoms are persistent, depression-related memory issues improve as mood lifts
  - **Supported.** Mirrors ICD-11 boundary guidance: concentration problems in depression "should not be considered part of the diagnosis of ADHD unless they have been present since childhood and persist after the resolution of the depressive episode".

- **Claim 8:** Both conditions are treatable
  - **Supported.** Consistent with NICE guideline treatment pathways and standard depression treatment literature.
